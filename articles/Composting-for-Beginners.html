Composting for Beginners

How to Start Composting

Composting turns kitchen scraps and garden waste into rich, dark soil that feeds your plants. It’s simple, eco‑friendly, and one of the best ways to improve your garden’s health.

A healthy compost pile full of kitchen scraps, leaves, and garden clippings.

What Is Compost?

Compost is decomposed organic matter—like food scraps, leaves, and plant trimmings. Microbes break these materials down into nutrient‑rich humus that improves soil structure, drainage, and fertility.

What You Can Compost

Composting works best when you balance “greens” (nitrogen‑rich materials) with “browns” (carbon‑rich materials).

Greens (Nitrogen)

  • Fruit & vegetable scraps
  • Coffee grounds
  • Fresh grass clippings
  • Plant trimmings

Browns (Carbon)

  • Dry leaves
  • Cardboard & paper
  • Straw
  • Wood chips

What NOT to Compost

These items slow decomposition, attract pests, or create unpleasant smells.

  • Meat, fish, or dairy
  • Oily foods
  • Pet waste
  • Diseased plants
  • Glossy or coated paper

How to Build a Compost Pile

Composting is easy when you follow a simple structure and keep the pile balanced.

1. Choose a Spot

Pick a corner of your garden or use a compost bin. Good airflow helps decomposition.

2. Add Browns First

Start with a layer of dry leaves or cardboard to help drainage and airflow.

3. Add Greens

Mix in kitchen scraps and fresh plant material. Keep layers loose, not compacted.

4. Keep Moist

The pile should feel like a wrung‑out sponge—not too wet, not too dry.

5. Turn Occasionally

Turning adds oxygen and speeds decomposition. Aim for every 2–3 weeks.

6. Wait & Harvest

Compost is ready when it’s dark, crumbly, and smells earthy—usually after 2–6 months.

Benefits of Composting

Compost improves soil structure, boosts plant growth, and reduces household waste.

  • Better drainage and aeration
  • More nutrients for plants
  • Reduced need for chemical fertilisers
  • Less waste going to landfill
